### §2.4 🔑 WHY THE WRONG CONCLUSION WAS REACHED — the transferable part

The 21:13 control was **run at 21:12, AFTER the 21:07–21:10 failure window had already closed.**
A control that is **not concurrent** with the failures cannot control for a **time-varying** confound —
it can only tell you the system works *now*. Against seven contiguous failures it looked like a clean
A/B on the binary axis, and the arithmetic even flattered it: at `A47`'s recorded ~2-in-5 rate,
`P(7 consecutive failures) ≈ 0.4^7 ≈ 0.16 %`, which reads as "not chance" — **but that computation
silently assumes the failures are independent, and clustering is precisely the hypothesis it was being
used to dismiss.**

⇒ **`G257`.** Interleaving is not a stylistic nicety; it is what makes the comparison a comparison.
`G186` already required `A,B,B,A` ordering for *warm-up* bias in a timing prior — this is the same rule
biting on a **validity gate**, where the cost of getting it wrong is attributing a bench flake to the
product and either shipping a fix for nothing or parking a good milestone.
